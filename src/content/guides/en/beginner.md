Welcome! Starting at the gym can feel confusing: so many machines, so many opinions, and everyone else seems to know what they are doing. The truth is that a beginner needs only a few simple things done consistently. This guide walks you through them step by step. It is general education, not a personal prescription — your coach and, where needed, your doctor can tailor it to you.

## Start with the right mindset

Your first goal is not to lift the heaviest weight in the gym. It is to build a habit, learn the main movements well, and leave each session feeling better than when you came in. Progress in the first months is often quick and motivating, as long as you stay consistent and patient. Think in months, not days.

For your first visits, keep it practical: wear comfortable clothes and proper training shoes, bring a water bottle and a small towel, and arrive with a short plan written on your phone or on paper. If you don't know how to adjust a machine's seat or handles, ask — setting it up correctly for your body matters more than the weight you put on it.

## How many days a week?

For people new to strength training, the American College of Sports Medicine suggests training **2–3 days a week**. A simple, effective approach is **2–3 full-body sessions** with at least one rest day between them, for example Saturday, Monday and Wednesday.

Each session can include:

- A squat or leg press movement.
- A hip hinge such as a Romanian deadlift or hip thrust.
- A push such as a chest press or push-up.
- A pull such as a lat pulldown or seated row.
- An optional core or carry exercise.

You can browse ideas in our [workouts](/en/workouts/) and [exercises](/en/exercises/) sections.

## Choosing a manageable weight

A good beginner weight is one you can lift with **perfect form** while finishing the set with about **2–3 reps still "in the tank"**. This idea is called *reps in reserve* (RIR): how many more good reps you could have done before failing. Researchers have described how this scale helps people match the load to how they feel on the day.

In practice:

- If the last rep was easy and you could have done many more, go a little heavier next time.
- If your form broke down or you couldn't finish, go lighter.
- Never trade form for a heavier number.

## How to perform a repetition

Each rep is a skill. Aim for:

- **Control:** lift with purpose, lower slowly and smoothly. No swinging or bouncing.
- **Full, safe range:** move through the range you can control without pain.
- **Stable position:** feet planted, trunk braced, and a steady breathing pattern.
- **Consistency:** every rep should look like the first one.

If you are not sure what correct form looks like, our exercise pages explain the key points, and filming a set from the side is a useful way to check yourself.

## Always warm up

Begin each session with **5–10 minutes of light-to-moderate activity**, as recommended by the American College of Sports Medicine — brisk walking, easy cycling or light bodyweight moves. Then do one or two lighter sets of your first exercise before your working sets. See the [warm-up exercises](/en/exercises/?category=warm-up) for ideas.

## Rest between sets

Rest about **1–3 minutes** between sets. Lighter, smaller exercises usually need less; heavy squats, presses or rows may need the longer end, or a little more. The ACSM position stand describes 1–2 minutes for muscle-building work and longer rests for heavy strength work. If you rush back too quickly, your form tends to suffer.

## Progressive overload: how you improve

Your body adapts when you gradually ask a bit more of it. For beginners, the simplest way is:

1. **Add reps first.** If your target is 8–12 reps, stay with the same weight until you can do 12 good reps on all sets.
2. **Then add a little weight** and drop back toward the lower rep number.
3. **Repeat.**

The ACSM suggests increasing the load by about **2–10%** once you can do one or two reps more than your target. Small jumps are fine — progress is still progress. Write your weights and reps down so you know what to beat next time.

## Recovery and rest days

Muscles grow and adapt between sessions, not during them. Take rest days, avoid training the same muscles hard on consecutive days, and expect some muscle soreness after new exercises. Soreness should fade within a few days. Sharp pain, joint pain or swelling is different and needs attention.

Outside the gym, keep moving: WHO advises adults to get **150–300 minutes of moderate activity per week** and to do muscle-strengthening activities on **2 or more days a week**. Walking on rest days counts.

## Sleep

Sleep is when much of your recovery happens. Adults are advised to sleep **7 or more hours per night on a regular basis**. Keep a steady bedtime, reduce screens late at night, and avoid strong caffeine close to bedtime.

## Nutrition basics

You don't need a perfect diet to start, but a few basics help a lot:

- **Protein at each meal:** chicken, fish, eggs, meat, laban, cheese, lentils, chickpeas. For people who train, the International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests about **1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day**.
- **Plenty of vegetables and fruit**, plus starches like rice (timman), bulgur and bread to fuel your training.
- **Water** through the day, especially in the summer heat.

To estimate your daily energy needs, try our [calorie calculator](/en/calculator/), and read more in our [nutrition articles](/en/nutrition/).

## Common beginner mistakes

- Lifting too heavy too soon (ego lifting).
- Skipping the warm-up.
- Training with no plan and changing programmes every week.
- Sleeping too little.
- Comparing yourself to people who have trained for years.
- Ignoring pain.

## When to ask a coach — and when to see a doctor

Ask Coach Rani Asmaro whenever you are unsure how to perform an exercise, how heavy to go, or how to adjust your plan. That is exactly what a coach is for.

See a doctor before starting, or before training harder, if you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, diabetes, a recent injury or surgery, a chronic condition, or if you are pregnant. Stop and seek medical advice if you feel chest pain, dizziness, unusual shortness of breath, or sharp or persistent pain during or after exercise.

## Your first steps

- Train 2–3 full-body days a week.
- Choose weights you can lift with perfect form, leaving 2–3 reps in reserve.
- Warm up, rest 1–3 minutes between sets, and write your numbers down.
- Add reps, then weight — slowly and steadily.
- Sleep 7+ hours, eat enough protein, and ask for help when you need it.
