A calorie is a unit of energy. Food labels usually show kilocalories (kcal), which is what people mean when they say "calories". Your body uses this energy for everything: breathing, keeping warm, digesting food, walking and training.

## Where your daily energy goes

- **Resting needs:** the energy that keeps your body running at rest — heart, brain, breathing and body temperature
- **Digestion:** a smaller amount used to digest and process food
- **Movement:** everyday activity such as walking, work and chores, plus exercise

## How needs are estimated

Researchers have developed equations that estimate your resting energy needs from your weight, height, age and sex. A widely used example is the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, published in 1990. That estimate is then multiplied by an activity factor to approximate your total daily needs.

You don't have to do the math yourself: use our [calorie calculator](/en/calculator/).

## Estimates vary

Every equation is built on averages from groups of people. Your real needs may be higher or lower because of your muscle mass, genetics, daily movement, sleep and more. Treat the result as a **starting point**, not an exact fact.

## How to use the number

1. Start with the estimate for your goal.
2. Weigh yourself a few mornings a week under the same conditions, and watch the trend over several weeks rather than single days.
3. Adjust your intake a little if your weight is not moving in the direction you want.

## Quality still matters

Two meals with the same calories can affect your hunger, energy and health very differently. A plate of chicken, bulgur and salad keeps you full far longer than the same calories from sweets or sugary drinks. Build most meals around protein, vegetables, whole grains and fruit.

## When to get help

If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have diabetes or another medical condition, or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before changing how much you eat. Avoid very low-calorie diets unless a doctor is supervising you.

## Bottom line

- Calories measure the energy in food and the energy your body uses.
- Calculators give an estimate — a starting point, not a final answer.
- Track your weight trend for several weeks and adjust gradually.
- The quality of your calories matters as much as the number.
