You train hard in the gym, but your body does much of its repair and adaptation while you rest. Good sleep supports your energy, mood, focus and your next workout. Poor sleep makes everything feel heavier.

## How much sleep do adults need?

A joint consensus statement from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the Sleep Research Society recommends that adults sleep **7 or more hours per night on a regular basis** for good health. Some people need more, for example when recovering from illness or a period of short sleep.

The key word is *regular*. One long night at the weekend does not fully make up for a week of short nights.

## Simple habits that help

- **Keep a steady schedule.** Go to bed and wake up at similar times, including on weekends.
- **Make the room dark, quiet and cool.** In the Iraqi summer, a fan or air conditioning and light covers can make a real difference.
- **Put screens away before bed.** Phones and late-night scrolling keep the mind busy. Try reading or a calm conversation instead.
- **Avoid a heavy meal right before sleeping.** A light dinner such as yoghurt (laban), eggs or a small plate of lentil soup sits better than a large late meal.
- **Get daylight and movement during the day.** Being active and outdoors in the morning helps your body clock.

## Caffeine and sleep

Tea, coffee and energy drinks all contain caffeine. The European Food Safety Authority notes that caffeine taken close to bedtime can make it harder to fall asleep and shorten sleep in some adults. If you train in the evening, think carefully before having a strong coffee or a pre-workout drink. Many people do better keeping caffeine to the earlier part of the day.

## Sleep and your training

When sleep is short, workouts often feel harder and motivation drops. If you had a bad night, it is fine to train a little lighter that day rather than chase a personal best.

If you regularly struggle to sleep, snore loudly, or feel very tired despite enough time in bed, speak to a doctor. These can be signs of a sleep problem that needs proper assessment.

## Bottom line

- Aim for 7 or more hours of sleep on a regular basis.
- Keep a steady schedule and a dark, cool, quiet room.
- Limit screens and caffeine close to bedtime.
- Persistent sleep problems deserve a visit to a doctor.
