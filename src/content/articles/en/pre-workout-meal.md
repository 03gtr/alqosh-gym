Eating before training gives you the energy to work hard and helps you avoid feeling weak or dizzy halfway through a session. It doesn't need to be complicated.

## When to eat

The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) describes a carbohydrate-based meal or snack eaten in the **1–4 hours before exercise**. For most gym members, a normal meal around 1–3 hours before training works well. The closer you are to your session, the smaller and lighter the meal should be.

## What to eat

- **Carbohydrates** as the main fuel: rice, bread or samoon, bulgur, potatoes, fruit, dates
- **Some protein:** eggs, chicken, yogurt, cheese or labneh. The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand on nutrient timing notes that protein eaten in the hours around training supports muscle repair and growth.
- **Moderate fat and fiber** right before training. Both digest slowly and, in large amounts, can upset your stomach during exercise.

## Meal ideas (2–3 hours before)

- Chicken with rice and a small salad
- Eggs with bread, cucumber and tomato
- Bulgur with yogurt
- Lentil soup with bread — if lentils or beans give you gas, eat them earlier in the day instead

## Light options (about 1 hour before)

- A banana or a few dates
- Yogurt with fruit
- A small samoon with cheese or labneh

## What to avoid right before training

- Heavy, fried or very fatty meals
- Very large portions
- Trying new foods before an important or hard session

## Training early in the morning

Some people train comfortably without eating, while others feel weak. If you train early, try a light snack such as a banana or a few dates, then eat a proper meal afterwards. Experiment to find what suits you.

If you have diabetes or take medication that lowers blood sugar, ask your doctor or a registered dietitian how to time your meals around training.

## Bottom line

- Eat a normal meal about 1–3 hours before training, or a light snack if time is short.
- Make carbohydrates the base and add some protein.
- Keep the meal before training light, familiar and not too fatty.
