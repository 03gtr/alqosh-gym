Water helps control your body temperature, carries nutrients and keeps your muscles working. Even mild dehydration can make training feel harder, and in hot weather it can become dangerous.

## Everyday hydration

There is no single number that fits everyone: your needs depend on your body size, activity and the weather. Drink regularly with meals and between them. Water is the best everyday choice, and laban, soups, cucumber, watermelon and other fruit also contribute.

## The urine color check

A simple daily check: pale yellow urine usually means you are well hydrated, while dark yellow means you should drink more. Some vitamins and foods can change the color, so look at the general pattern.

## Before training

The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) suggests drinking about **5–7 mL per kg of body weight at least 4 hours before exercise** — roughly 350–500 mL for a 70 kg person. This gives your body time to absorb what it needs and pass the rest.

## During training

Sip water during your session. ACSM's goal is to avoid losing more than **2% of your body weight** through sweat, because larger losses can reduce performance. To learn how much you sweat, weigh yourself before and after a session in similar clothes.

## After training

If you lost weight during training, ACSM suggests drinking about **1.5 L of fluid for every 1 kg lost**, spread over the following hours, together with your normal meals. The salt in food helps your body hold on to the fluid.

## Iraqi summer heat

- Train in the cooler hours of the day when possible.
- Start your session already hydrated.
- Wear light, breathable clothes and take short breaks.
- Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, confused, very weak or nauseous, or have a severe headache. Move to the shade, cool down, and seek medical help if you don't improve quickly.

## Don't overdrink

Drinking much more than you sweat can dilute the sodium in your blood (hyponatremia), which can be dangerous. Drink to replace what you lose — don't force large amounts.

If you have heart or kidney disease or have been told to limit fluids, follow your doctor's advice.

## Bottom line

- Drink regularly and use urine color as a simple guide.
- Start training hydrated and sip during the session.
- Replace what you lose after training — but don't overdrink.
