Caffeine is found in coffee, tea, energy drinks, some soft drinks and many pre-workout products. It can make training feel easier — but more is not better. This article is for healthy adults.

## Can it help performance?

The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand on caffeine (2021) concludes that caffeine can improve many types of exercise performance, including endurance and some strength and power tasks. The effect differs from person to person. Studies typically use about **3–6 mg per kg of body weight**, taken around **60 minutes before exercise**, and lower doses can also work for some people.

## How much is safe?

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) concluded that, for healthy adults:

- Up to **400 mg per day** from all sources does not raise safety concerns.
- Single doses up to **200 mg** do not raise safety concerns, including when taken shortly before intense exercise.
- For pregnant and breastfeeding women, up to **200 mg per day** does not raise concerns for the baby.

Note that the higher research doses can go beyond these limits for heavier people — for example, 6 mg/kg for an 80 kg person is 480 mg. Start low, and keep your total for the whole day within the limit. Caffeine content varies a lot between drinks and products, so read labels.

## Protect your sleep

EFSA notes that even 100 mg of caffeine close to bedtime can make it harder to fall asleep and shorten sleep in some adults. Avoid caffeine in the late afternoon and evening, especially if you train in the evening. Poor sleep harms your recovery far more than caffeine helps your workout.

## Be careful with energy drinks

- Many contain caffeine plus a lot of sugar.
- It's easy to drink several cans and go over the limit.
- Never mix them with alcohol.
- Children and teenagers should avoid them.

## Who should ask a doctor first

If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, anxiety, sleep problems or stomach problems, or you take regular medication, ask your doctor before using caffeine for training.

## Bottom line

- Caffeine can modestly help performance, but it's optional.
- Healthy adults: stay within 400 mg per day; pregnant or breastfeeding: within 200 mg per day.
- Avoid it in the late afternoon and evening to protect your sleep.
- Treat energy drinks with caution.
