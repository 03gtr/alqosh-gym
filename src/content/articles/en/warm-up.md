Walking into the gym and loading the bar straight away is tempting, but a few minutes of warming up makes your session feel better and helps you train with good technique from the first set.

## Why warm up?

- It gradually raises your heart rate and body temperature.
- It gets your joints moving through the ranges you are about to use.
- It lets you practise the movement pattern before the weights get heavy.
- It helps you focus and switch from "work mode" to "training mode".

## Part 1: general warm-up (5–10 minutes)

The American College of Sports Medicine recommends a warm-up of **at least 5–10 minutes of light-to-moderate activity** before exercise. Simple options:

- Brisk walking or easy cycling on the bike.
- Rowing machine or cross-trainer at an easy pace.
- Light bodyweight moves: arm circles, leg swings, hip circles, bodyweight squats.

You should feel warmer and slightly out of breath — not tired.

## Part 2: specific warm-up sets

Before your first main exercise, do a few lighter sets of the same movement. For example, before squats:

- An empty bar or a very light weight for several easy, smooth reps.
- A moderate weight for fewer reps.
- A heavier weight for just a couple of reps, still clearly lighter than your working weight.

Then start your working sets. Lighter exercises later in the session usually need only one quick warm-up set.

You can find ideas in our [warm-up exercises](/en/exercises/?category=warm-up).

## Common warm-up mistakes

- **Skipping it** when short on time — even a short warm-up is better than none.
- **Making it exhausting** — the warm-up should prepare you, not tire you out.
- **Only doing long, still stretches** — before lifting, active movements usually fit better; longer stretching holds can be done after training.

If something hurts during your warm-up (sharp pain, not just stiffness), don't push through it. Ask a coach, and see a doctor or physiotherapist if it persists.

## Bottom line

- Start with 5–10 minutes of light, general activity.
- Add a few lighter warm-up sets before your main lifts.
- Keep it short and energising, not exhausting.
- Pain during the warm-up is a signal to stop and get advice.
