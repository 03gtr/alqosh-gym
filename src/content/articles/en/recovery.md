Training is the signal; recovery is when your body answers it. If you train hard but recover poorly, progress stalls and the risk of feeling run down goes up. Recovery is not laziness — it is part of the plan.

## Rest days

Your muscles, joints and nervous system need time between hard sessions. Plan regular rest days into your week, and avoid training the same muscles hard on back-to-back days. A rest day can still include a relaxed walk or light stretching.

## Sleep comes first

Sleep is one of the most important recovery tools you have. Adults are advised to get **7 or more hours per night on a regular basis** (AASM/SRS consensus). If your sleep is short, extra supplements or gadgets will not make up for it. See our sleep article for practical tips.

## Eat enough, and include protein

Recovery needs building materials and energy.

- **Protein:** the International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests about **1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day** for most people who exercise, spread across the day. Good local options include chicken, fish, eggs, meat, laban, cheese, lentils and chickpeas.
- **Carbohydrates:** rice (timman), bread, bulgur, potatoes, dates and fruit help refill energy for the next session.
- **Water:** drink regularly, especially in hot weather.

## Lighter weeks (deload)

After several weeks of hard training, some programmes include a planned lighter week with fewer sets or lighter weights. This can help you feel fresh again. Beginners often don't need one early on, but it is a useful tool as training gets harder.

## Soreness vs pain

- **Normal soreness:** a dull, general ache in the muscles you trained, usually a day or two after a new or harder workout. It eases with gentle movement and fades within a few days.
- **Warning signs:** sharp or stabbing pain, pain in a joint, swelling, pain that gets worse during exercise, numbness, or pain that doesn't improve. Stop that exercise and see a doctor or a qualified physiotherapist.

## Bottom line

- Plan rest days and avoid hammering the same muscles every day.
- Protect your sleep: 7+ hours regularly.
- Eat enough, with protein spread through the day.
- Know the difference between soreness and pain, and get help for pain.
