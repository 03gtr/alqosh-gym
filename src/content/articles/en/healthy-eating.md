Healthy eating does not need special foods or strict rules. It is mostly about variety, sensible portions and choosing less-processed food most of the time. The good news: many traditional Iraqi foods fit perfectly.

## The plate method

A simple way to build a meal without counting anything:

- **Half the plate: vegetables and salad.** Cucumber, tomato, lettuce, cooked vegetables, a vegetable stew.
- **A quarter: protein.** Chicken, fish, eggs, lean meat, lentils, chickpeas, white beans or broad beans (baqilla).
- **A quarter: starch.** Rice (timman), bulgur, bread or samoon — wholegrain when you can.
- **Add:** a little healthy fat such as olive oil, and a piece of fruit or a glass of laban on the side.

Adjust portions to your goals and activity level. For a personal estimate of your daily energy needs, try our [calorie calculator](/en/calculator/).

## Vegetables and fruit every day

The World Health Organization advises adults to eat **at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables a day** (about five portions). Seasonal fruit, a salad with lunch and vegetables in stews make this easy. Dates are a traditional sweet option too — just mind the portion, as they are energy-dense.

## Limit sugar and sugary drinks

WHO recommends keeping free sugars to **less than 10% of daily energy**. Much of this sugar hides in drinks: soft drinks, sweetened juices, energy drinks and very sweet tea. Choosing water, unsweetened tea or laban is one of the easiest changes you can make.

## Watch salt and fats

- WHO advises adults to eat **less than 5 g of salt a day** (just under a teaspoon). Pickles, processed meats, salty cheese, crisps and stock cubes add up quickly.
- Prefer unsaturated fats such as olive oil, nuts and fish over butter, ghee and fatty processed meats, and avoid industrial trans fats found in some fried and packaged snacks.

## Variety beats perfection

No single food makes or breaks your health. An occasional celebration meal is part of life. What matters is your usual pattern across weeks and months.

If you have diabetes, kidney disease, high blood pressure, allergies or are pregnant, ask a doctor or a registered dietitian for advice that fits your situation.

## Bottom line

- Fill half your plate with vegetables, a quarter with protein, a quarter with starch.
- Eat at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables daily.
- Cut back on sugary drinks and salty processed foods.
- Focus on your usual pattern, not one perfect meal.
