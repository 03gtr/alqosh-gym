After training, your body starts repairing muscle and refilling its energy stores. A good meal helps that process — but it doesn't need to be rushed, expensive or special.

## Your total daily intake matters most

The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand on nutrient timing says the main priority is meeting your **total daily protein intake**, ideally spread evenly across the day. Meal timing is a fine-tuning detail: a perfect post-workout meal cannot make up for a poor overall diet.

## Protein

The ISSN recommends about **20–40 g of protein per meal**. Its nutrient timing position stand notes that high-quality protein eaten from right after training up to about 2 hours afterwards supports muscle repair and growth. There is no need to panic if you eat a little later — just make sure your next meal contains good protein.

## Carbohydrates

Carbs refill the glycogen (stored energy) you used during training. This matters most if you train hard, or if you will train again later the same day or early the next morning. Pair your protein with rice, bread, potatoes, bulgur or fruit.

## Practical meals

- Chicken or meat with rice and salad
- Eggs with bread, labneh, cucumber and tomato
- Fish with potatoes and salad
- Lentil soup with bread and a glass of laban
- Yogurt with fruit and a few dates — a lighter option

## Do you need a shake?

Not necessarily. Protein powder is simply a convenient option for when you can't eat a meal soon after training. A normal meal does the same job.

## Don't forget fluids

Drink water after your session to replace what you lost in sweat, especially in summer.

If your doctor has asked you to limit protein — for example because of kidney disease — follow that advice, or ask a registered dietitian to plan your meals.

## Bottom line

- Hitting your daily protein total matters more than exact timing.
- Aim for a meal with 20–40 g of protein plus carbs within a couple of hours of training.
- Simple local meals work perfectly; a shake is optional.
