Good technique is not about looking perfect. It is about moving in a way that trains the right muscles, lets you progress steadily, and keeps your joints happy for years. Here are the basics that apply to almost every exercise.

## Control the weight

You should move the weight — the weight should not move you. If you have to swing, bounce or jerk the load to finish a rep, it is probably too heavy for now. Lower the weight under control instead of letting it drop.

## Use a full, safe range of motion

Move through the full range that you can control without pain. Cutting reps short just to use a heavier weight may feel impressive, but it makes the load harder to control and your real progress harder to track. Your safe range depends on your body and mobility, so don't force positions that hurt.

## Keep a steady tempo

A simple rule for beginners: lift with purpose, lower with control, and avoid bouncing at the bottom. A steady rhythm keeps tension on the muscle and makes it easier to notice when form starts to break down.

## Brace your trunk

Before heavy squats, deadlifts, rows or presses, take a breath and tighten your stomach muscles as if preparing for a light punch. This helps keep your spine stable. Avoid holding your breath for long periods, and if you have high blood pressure, heart problems or are pregnant, ask your doctor which breathing and effort levels are safe for you.

## Film yourself

What you feel and what you actually do are often different. Record a set from the side with your phone and compare it with our [exercise pages](/en/exercises/). Look at your back position, depth and how the bar or dumbbells move.

## Ask a coach

A coach can spot problems quickly and give you one or two simple cues to fix them. Asking is part of learning, not a sign of weakness.

## When technique breaks down

If your form gets noticeably worse near the end of a set, stop the set there. That last ugly rep is rarely worth it. If an exercise causes sharp pain, joint pain or numbness, stop and see a doctor or physiotherapist.

## Bottom line

- Control the weight on the way up and on the way down.
- Use the full range you can control without pain.
- Brace your trunk on big lifts, and film yourself to check.
- Ask a coach when unsure, and stop when form breaks down.
