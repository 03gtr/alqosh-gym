Carbohydrates are your muscles' main fuel during hard training. Your body stores them in the muscles and liver as glycogen and draws on that store during weight training, running and intense classes. When the store is low, workouts feel heavier and harder to finish.

## How much do you need?

It depends on how much you train. The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) joint position statement suggests about **3–5 g of carbohydrate per kg of body weight per day** for light or skill-based training, and more for people who train longer or harder. Most gym members do not need extreme amounts — regular meals that each include a good carb source are usually enough.

## Quality matters

A large series of reviews published in The Lancet (Reynolds and colleagues, 2019) linked higher intakes of fiber and whole grains with lower rates of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer. So the type of carbohydrate matters, not just the amount.

Choose more often:

- Bulgur, oats and brown or whole-wheat bread
- Rice — portion it to your needs and pair it with vegetables and protein
- Potatoes, boiled or baked rather than fried
- Lentils, chickpeas and beans
- Seasonal fruit and dates

Limit:

- Sugary drinks, sweets and white-flour pastries

The World Health Organization (WHO) advises keeping free sugars below 10% of your total daily energy intake.

## Around training

A meal with carbs a few hours before training helps you perform well. If you are short on time, a few dates or a banana make a quick, easy snack. After training, carbs help refill your glycogen, especially if you will train again soon.

## If you have diabetes

If you have diabetes or prediabetes, work with your doctor or a registered dietitian to plan your carbohydrate amounts and timing around training.

## Bottom line

- Carbs are the main fuel for hard training.
- Pick whole grains, legumes, fruit and vegetables most of the time.
- Keep sugary drinks and sweets low.
