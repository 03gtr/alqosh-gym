Fat is not the enemy. Your body needs it to absorb vitamins A, D, E and K, to build cell membranes and hormones, and as a long-lasting source of energy. The key is choosing the right types and keeping the amounts sensible.

## Favor unsaturated fats

The World Health Organization (WHO) says unsaturated fats — found in fish, avocado, nuts and vegetable oils such as olive oil — are preferable to saturated fats. Good everyday choices:

- Olive oil on salads and in cooking
- A handful of nuts, such as walnuts or almonds
- Grilled or baked fish
- Tahini (sesame paste) in moderate amounts

## Limit saturated and trans fats

WHO guidance for adults:

- **Total fat:** no more than 30% of daily energy intake
- **Saturated fat:** no more than 10% of energy — found in fatty meat, butter, ghee and full-fat cheese
- **Trans fat:** no more than 1% of energy — found mainly in industrially produced foods such as some fried fast food, packaged snacks, biscuits and pastries

You do not need to calculate percentages every day. Simply make unsaturated fats your main choice and keep the others occasional.

## Watch the portion

Fat is the most energy-dense nutrient, so it adds calories quickly. A heavy pour of oil in the pan or a big bowl of nuts can add a lot of energy without filling you up much. Measuring oil with a spoon instead of pouring freely is a simple habit that helps.

## Practical tips

- Grill, bake or boil more often than deep-frying.
- Trim visible fat from meat, and remove chicken skin when you want a lighter meal.
- Use yogurt or labneh with herbs instead of heavy creamy sauces.
- Check food labels for "partially hydrogenated oils", a common source of trans fat.

If you have high cholesterol, heart disease or diabetes, follow your doctor's or a registered dietitian's advice on how much and which fats to eat.

## Bottom line

- Your body needs fat — don't cut it out completely.
- Favor olive oil, nuts and fish.
- Limit fatty meat, butter, ghee, and fried or packaged foods.
- Measure your oil: fat adds up fast.
