Protein gives your body the building blocks (amino acids) it uses to repair and build muscle after training. It also helps you feel full, which is useful whether your goal is to gain muscle or lose fat.

## How much per day?

The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) recommends about **1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day** for most people who exercise. For a 70 kg person, that is roughly 100–140 g a day.

A large review of many studies (Morton and colleagues) found that, in healthy adults doing resistance training, eating more protein stopped adding further muscle gains at around 1.6 g/kg/day on average. More is not always better — reaching your daily target consistently matters most.

## Spread it across the day

The ISSN also suggests spreading protein across your meals: about 20–40 g per meal, every 3–4 hours. In practice, that means 3–4 meals that each contain a clear protein source.

## Local food sources

- Eggs — an easy breakfast option
- Chicken, fish and lean meat
- Yogurt (laban), labneh and cheese
- Lentils, chickpeas, beans and fava beans — pair them with bread or bulgur for a better mix
- Nuts — less protein, but they add healthy fats

An example day: eggs with bread in the morning, chicken with rice and a cucumber-and-tomato salad at lunch, lentil soup with yogurt at dinner, and a handful of nuts as a snack.

## Do you need protein powder?

No. Protein powder is simply a convenient food. It can help if you struggle to reach your target from meals, but everyday foods do the same job.

## A safety note

If you have kidney disease, diabetes or another chronic condition, or you are pregnant, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before increasing your protein intake.

## Bottom line

- Aim for about 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day if you train regularly.
- Spread your protein over 3–4 meals.
- Local everyday foods can cover your needs; powder is optional.
