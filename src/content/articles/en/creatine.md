Creatine is one of the most studied sports supplements. It is a natural compound that your body makes itself and that you also get from meat and fish. It is **not** a steroid and not a hormone.

## What it does

Your muscles store creatine and use it to quickly regenerate energy during short, intense efforts such as lifting weights or sprinting. The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand (Kreider and colleagues, 2017) considers creatine monohydrate the most effective nutritional supplement currently available for increasing high-intensity exercise capacity and lean muscle mass during training. It helps you train a little harder — it does not replace good training and nutrition.

## Which form?

Creatine monohydrate has by far the most research behind it. Other forms have not been shown to work better.

## How adults typically take it

- **Simple method:** 3–5 g per day. Muscle stores rise gradually over about 3–4 weeks.
- **Faster method (optional):** a short "loading" phase of about 0.3 g per kg of body weight per day (roughly 20 g a day for a 70 kg person, split into several smaller doses) for a few days, then 3–5 g per day.

Take it at any time of day — with a meal is fine. Taking it consistently matters more than the exact timing. Drink water normally through the day.

## Is it safe?

According to the ISSN, short- and long-term creatine use at recommended doses is safe and well tolerated in healthy people. Research has not found that it causes dehydration, cramps or kidney damage in healthy people. You may gain a little weight at first, mostly from extra water stored inside your muscles.

Creatine can raise creatinine, a blood marker doctors use to check kidney function. If you have a blood test, tell your doctor that you take creatine.

## Who should ask first

- People with kidney disease, or at higher risk of it (for example with diabetes or high blood pressure)
- Anyone taking regular medication
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women

These groups should talk to a doctor before using creatine. This advice is for adults. If you choose to use it, pick a product that has been tested by an independent third party.

## Bottom line

- Creatine monohydrate is well researched and is not a steroid.
- A typical dose is 3–5 g per day; loading is optional.
- It is safe for healthy adults at recommended doses.
- Ask your doctor first if you have kidney problems or other health conditions.
