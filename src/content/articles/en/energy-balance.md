Over time, your body weight depends on energy balance: the energy you take in from food and drinks compared with the energy your body uses.

## The basics

- **Deficit:** you take in less than you use, and weight goes down over time.
- **Surplus:** you take in more than you use, and weight goes up.
- **Maintenance:** intake and use are roughly equal, and weight stays stable.

## It's not a fixed equation

Research by Hall and colleagues, published in The Lancet in 2011, showed that the body adapts. As you lose weight, your body needs less energy, so the same intake produces slower and slower loss. Popular rules of thumb that promise a fixed amount of weight loss for a fixed calorie deficit overestimate long-term results. That is why progress naturally slows and plateaus happen — it doesn't mean you have failed.

## Why the scale jumps around

Day-to-day changes are mostly water, not fat:

- Salty meals make your body hold more water.
- Carbohydrates are stored together with water, so a high-carb day can raise the scale.
- Food and drink still in your gut add weight.
- Hormonal changes, such as the menstrual cycle, can cause water retention.
- Hard or new training can cause temporary water retention in muscles.

Weigh yourself under the same conditions (for example, in the morning after using the toilet) and focus on the weekly trend.

## A sustainable deficit or surplus

- **For fat loss:** choose a moderate deficit you can keep for months, with enough protein and strength training to protect your muscle. Extreme diets are hard to stick to and make it harder to keep your muscle.
- **For muscle gain:** use a small surplus together with progressive training. A large surplus mostly adds fat.

## When progress stalls

First check your consistency: weekends, sugary drinks, cooking oil and snacks add up quietly. Then make a small change, such as eating slightly less or walking more each day. Patience beats big, sudden changes.

If you have a medical condition, take medication that affects weight or appetite, or are pregnant, plan any weight change with your doctor or a registered dietitian.

## Bottom line

- Weight change follows energy balance over weeks, not days.
- The body adapts, so progress slows over time — that's normal.
- Judge progress by the trend, not a single weigh-in.
- Choose a moderate, sustainable deficit or surplus.
