Electrolytes are minerals — mainly sodium, potassium, chloride and magnesium — that carry an electrical charge in your body. They help control fluid balance, nerve signals and muscle contraction.

## What you lose in sweat

Sweat is mostly water, plus electrolytes — mainly sodium. The amount varies a lot from person to person. Some people are "salty sweaters": you may notice white salt marks on their clothes after training.

## Plain water is usually enough

For a typical gym session, plain water plus your normal meals replaces what you lose. Food is your main source of electrolytes, and everyday meals already contain salt. Most people do not need special electrolyte drinks or salt tablets for short workouts.

## When you may need more

- Long sessions with heavy sweating, especially in summer heat
- Training twice in one day
- Heavy sweating when you can't eat a meal soon afterwards

In these situations, the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) notes that drinks containing electrolytes and carbohydrate can help, and that sodium from food or drinks after exercise helps your body hold on to the fluid you drink.

## Food first

- **Sodium:** a normal meal with bread, cheese or soup; a glass of laban with a pinch of salt
- **Potassium:** dates, bananas, potatoes, tomatoes, lentils, beans and yogurt

## Don't overdo salt

The World Health Organization (WHO) advises adults to keep salt **below 5 g per day** (about 2 g of sodium), and suggests a potassium intake of **at least 3,510 mg per day** may be beneficial. Many people already eat plenty of salt, so the usual priority is more fruit, vegetables and legumes — not extra salt.

If you have high blood pressure, heart disease or kidney disease, or take medication that affects fluids or electrolytes, talk to your doctor before changing your salt or potassium intake or using electrolyte products.

## Bottom line

- Electrolytes keep your fluids, nerves and muscles working.
- For most sessions, water and normal meals are enough.
- Long, sweaty sessions in the heat may need extra sodium from food or drinks.
- Don't add salt "just in case" — most diets already have enough.
