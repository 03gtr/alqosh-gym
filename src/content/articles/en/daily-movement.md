An hour in the gym is great, but what you do in the other hours of the day matters too. Many of us sit for long periods: at work, in the car, in front of the TV or on the phone. Adding more movement to your day supports your health, your energy and your training.

## How much activity do adults need?

The World Health Organization's 2020 guidelines advise adults to do, each week:

- **150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity activity**, such as brisk walking, or
- **75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity**, such as running, or an equivalent mix of both.
- **Muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week** that work all the major muscle groups — this is where your gym training fits in.

Older adults are also advised to include activities that challenge balance and strength. If you are just starting, don't worry about the full target: WHO stresses that some activity is better than none, and you can build up gradually.

## What counts as moderate or vigorous?

- **Moderate:** you breathe faster but can still talk comfortably — brisk walking, easy cycling, carrying shopping, active housework.
- **Vigorous:** you breathe hard and can only say a few words — running, fast cycling, hard circuit training.

## Sit less, move more

WHO also advises adults to limit the time spent sitting and to replace some of it with activity of any intensity, even light. The American College of Sports Medicine likewise suggests breaking up long periods of sitting with short bouts of standing and movement. Simple ideas:

- Stand up and walk for a few minutes every so often during work or study.
- Take phone calls while walking.
- Walk to the shop, the church, the mosque or a friend's house when it's safe and close.
- Take the stairs instead of the lift.
- Go for a walk with family or friends — in the summer heat, choose early morning or after sunset.

## Walking: the easiest start

Walking needs no equipment and suits almost everyone. Start where you are and add a little more over the weeks.

If you have heart disease, diabetes, joint problems, are pregnant, or have been inactive for a long time, check with your doctor about the right type and amount of activity for you.

## Bottom line

- Aim for 150–300 minutes of moderate activity (or 75–150 minutes vigorous) per week.
- Do muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week.
- Break up long periods of sitting.
- Any movement is better than none — start small and build up.
