Fiber is the part of plant foods that your body cannot fully digest. It supports regular digestion, helps you feel full for longer, and is linked with better long-term health. Many people focus on protein and forget about it.

## Why it matters

A large series of reviews in The Lancet (Reynolds and colleagues, 2019) found that people who ate the most fiber had lower rates of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer than those who ate the least. Fiber also helps with appetite control, which is useful when you are trying to lose fat.

## How much?

The World Health Organization (WHO) advises everyone older than 10 years to aim for **at least 25 g of naturally occurring fiber per day**, and at least 400 g of fruits and vegetables per day.

## Local sources

- **Vegetables:** cucumber, tomato, leafy greens, okra, eggplant, carrots
- **Legumes:** lentils, chickpeas, beans and fava beans
- **Whole grains:** bulgur, oats and whole-wheat bread
- **Fruit:** seasonal fruit (eaten with the skin when possible) and dates
- **Nuts and seeds**

## Simple swaps

- White bread → whole-wheat or brown bread
- Add a salad to lunch and dinner
- Lentil soup or a bean dish a few times a week
- Fruit instead of sweets after a meal
- Part of your rice replaced with bulgur

## Increase slowly and drink water

Adding a lot of fiber all at once can cause bloating and gas. Increase it gradually over a couple of weeks so your gut can adjust. Drink enough water through the day, because fiber works best with enough fluid.

If you have a digestive condition such as irritable bowel syndrome or inflammatory bowel disease, ask your doctor or a registered dietitian which fiber sources suit you.

## Bottom line

- Aim for at least 25 g of fiber a day from whole foods.
- Fill up on vegetables, legumes, whole grains and fruit.
- Increase slowly and drink water with it.
